Frozen Shoulder

Patience plus the right treatment — not one without the other.

Frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis) progressively stiffens the joint capsule: first pain, then striking loss of motion — reaching a shelf, fastening a seatbelt, sleeping on that side all become difficult.

Why it happens

The capsule inflames and contracts, sometimes after injury or immobilization, more often without clear cause. It’s more common in women over 40 and with diabetes or thyroid conditions.

How we treat it

Treatment matches the stage: calming pain early, then progressively restoring motion with joint mobilization and specific stretching as the shoulder allows. Honest expectation-setting matters — recovery takes months, and we track measurable range so you see it happening.

When to come in

Book when shoulder stiffness is progressing or motion loss appears — early-stage treatment shortens the course. Already months in? Rehab still speeds the thaw. No referral is needed — book online or call 647-710-6927.
